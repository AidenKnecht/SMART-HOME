# Clinician interview questions (8:40–10:30, Homeroom, 4th floor)

Bring this up on a phone. Capture **exact quotes**: they score under "use of clinical input."
Show them a quick sketch if we have one and ask "what's wrong with this?"

## First 30 seconds
1. When you open a patient's home data before a visit, what's the first thing you look for?
2. What would make you change treatment? What's noise you'd never look at?
3. How far back do you look: last 2 weeks, last month, since the last visit?

## Control and symptoms
4. How do you judge asthma control today: GINA criteria, the ACT score, gut feel?
5. A teen logs "Bad," then "Great" 4 minutes later. Which counts: worst of the day, latest, or both?
6. Patient says "Great" but reports wheezing. How should we show that conflict?
7. Do night symptoms or missed school matter more than daytime symptoms?

## Medications
8. How much do you trust self-reported doses? Is "% of controller doses logged" useful or misleading?
9. Rescue vs controller: what do you want to see for each?

## Spirometry
10. What do you look at first: FEV1, % predicted, peak flow, FEV1/FVC?
11. Rejected blows (failed quality check): hide them, fade them, or treat them as a technique problem?
12. Is a trend line more useful than individual readings?

## In the visit
13. Would you turn the screen toward the patient? What would you show a 14-year-old?
14. Who looks at this: you, an NP, a nurse? Does it need to fit inside Epic's SMART@Home tab?

## Other specialties and trackers
15. What would a GI doctor or neurologist want to see differently with a different tracker?
16. Would you want alerts between visits (e.g., 3 red-zone days), or is that a liability?

## Wrap-up
17. If this screen did one thing well, what should it be?
18. Can we quote you in our pitch (by role, not name)?
